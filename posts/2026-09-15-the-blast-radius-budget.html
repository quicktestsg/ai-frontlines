The Blast Radius Budget

Every capability granted to an agent has a blast radius: the set of states that cannot be restored if the action is wrong. The variable that determines whether an incident is a footnote or an outage is not the model. It is how much irreversibility one wrong step can spend.

Agent incidents are almost never interesting failures of intelligence. The model usually does roughly what it was asked. The interesting part is what the harness let the roughly-right action touch.

Not all actions weigh the same. The budget line is a design decision: actions past it must earn their way through a human signature or a two-phase apply.

Two-phase apply, or: the agent proposes, the harness disposes

The single highest-leverage pattern in agent safety is separating the decision to act from the act itself. The agent emits an intention; the harness is the only party able to convert intention into effect.

An agent's confidence is not a signature. The harness's validation is. Systems that conflate the two are not autonomous — they are uninsured.

The two-phase pattern: intentions are artifacts, not effects. The harness gate dry-runs, checks policy, collects signatures past budget, and only then does the world change.

Permission creep is the compound interest of laziness

Blast radius has a failure mode that arrives quietly: creep. Each individual grant is defensible; the sum is indefensible. Every capability should have an owner, a reason, and an expiry.

Creep is the quiet failure mode: each grant defensible alone, the sum indefensible. Price permissions by their worst day, not their usage rate.

Errors are constant; blast radius is chosen. Databases, filesystems, and deployment platforms became trustworthy not by becoming infallible but by making their failures small, staged, and reversible. Agents are next in line.
