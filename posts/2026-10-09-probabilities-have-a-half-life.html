Probabilities Have a Half-Life

Every belief in an agent plan was true when it was measured. The world edits the evidence afterward — and the plan never hears about it.

Picture a planning agent mid-task. At step one it assembled a portfolio of beliefs — each an honest summary of evidence at the moment of measurement. Then the loop kept running, and every one of those quietly became a claim about the past. Numbers in context don't wrinkle.

This is not about clocks — an agent can read a clock fine. It is that every probability is indexed to an evidence state, and evidence states do not announce their own obsolescence. Caches have TTLs; agent beliefs go into context bare, undated, looking fresh forever.

Solid curve: a belief that collapsed when the world intervened. Dashed: a belief decaying with entropy. The red box is what context still asserts at act time. That gap is the unpriced risk.

Three clocks, one loop

Staleness is three problems on three clocks. Evidence decay drifts (entropy), intervention decay snaps (events), and self-decay — the one never engineered — is when the agent's own actions falsify its own earlier measurements.

Evidence decay: smooth drift, treat with cheap re-verify. Intervention decay: a snap, treat with subscriptions. Self-decay: the agent's own writes falsify its own measurements — almost never engineered.

Date every number

The mechanic is small: every belief enters context wrapped in a record — what was observed, when, and what would change it. A structure the harness reads, not prose. The timestamp enables staleness arithmetic; the trigger enables subscription; the record enables self-decay accounting.

A number without a timestamp is not a fact about the world. It is a fact about a moment — and the loop was not built for that moment. It was built for this one.

Every consequential action passes a freshness gate: elapsed time versus decay class, plus the event feed for snaps. Fresh — act. Stale — re-measure, mint a new dated record.

Faster models do not dissolve this: agents are pointed at longer horizons, and even an instant loop reads sources that lag. The half-life of a probability is a property of the world, not the loop. Budget for decay — re-measure before the moments where being wrong stops being free.
