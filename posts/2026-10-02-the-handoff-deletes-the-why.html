The Handoff Deletes the Why

Multi-agent systems pass work the way relay runners pass a baton: the artifact arrives, and everything the runner was thinking does not.

Watch a multi-agent system in production and pay attention to the seams. Agent One spends forty tool calls investigating a problem, weighs three hypotheses, rejects two of them for reasons that were locally excellent, and hands a summary to Agent Two. The summary says what was found and what was concluded. It does not say what was rejected, or how sure Agent One was, or which assumptions the conclusion quietly leans on.

Agent One's working state is rich: hypotheses considered and rejected, calibrated confidence, load-bearing assumptions, questions left open. The handoff contract transmits exactly one row — the conclusion — and everything else is dropped.

Conclusions launder into facts

There is a quiet semantic drift that happens inside every pipeline of agents, and it compounds. When Agent One states a conclusion, that statement is, properly understood, a probabilistic claim wearing specific clothes. By the time the claim reaches Agent Three — summarized by Two, folded into a plan, cited as prior work — the clothes are gone.

This matters because the downstream agent is often the one holding the tools to refute the conclusion. Agent Two might have access to a check that would have falsified Agent One's guess in one call — but falsification is only attempted against claims that look falsifiable, and a naked conclusion doesn't.

Each handoff launders a little more uncertainty out of the claim. By the third hop the pipeline has minted a fact, and no verification occurred anywhere along the way — only transmission.

The rejected alternatives were the expensive part

There is an accounting error hiding in most handoff designs. The refutations are the most expensive item on the invoice and the only one with negative space — they describe the shape of what the answer is not. The system paid full price for the map and shipped the postcard.

The summary is not the work. The work is the space of paths not taken, carved away one refutation at a time. A handoff that transmits only the destination forces every downstream agent to re-derive the journey — and they will re-derive it worse.

The fix is a rationale ledger, not a bigger window

The reflexive fix is to ship the whole transcript — dump Agent One's full context into Agent Two's window. This rarely works. The transcript is chronological, not structured; the why is scattered across forty interleaved turns. A bigger window buys the ability to re-perform the archaeology, which is different from receiving the findings.

The envelope is deliberately small: conclusion with confidence, rejected paths with reasons, unverified assumptions, and the falsifiers that would flip the call. It gives the receiver the two capabilities a naked summary destroys — the ability to challenge the inference, and the ability to skip the work that was already done.

Trust is downstream of traceability

The deepest cost of the why-less handoff shows up when a human finally inspects the system. A multi-agent pipeline that transmits conclusions without provenance is a black box with extra steps. The operator's only safe move is to distrust everything — the system saved compute and spent trust.
