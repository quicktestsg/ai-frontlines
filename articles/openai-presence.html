OpenAI Presence: Enterprise Voice & Chat Agents

A new platform for deploying trusted AI agents inside the enterprise — with the one feature that matters most to large customers: escalation.

Enterprise AI has a recurring awkwardness. Every vendor pitches the same vision: autonomous agents that handle customer support, internal IT, and operational workflows at scale. And yet, when you ask CIOs what actually happens in production, the answer is almost always the same — the agents handle the easy 60%, hallucinate on the difficult 20%, and humans clean up the messy remainder at greater cost than if they'd done the whole thing themselves. OpenAI's new Presence platform is a direct attempt to fix that economics problem.

Built for eligible enterprise customers under limited general availability, Presence lets organizations deploy voice and chat agents across customer-facing and internal workflows. The agents answer questions, pull information from company systems, take approved actions, and — critically — escalate to human colleagues when confidence drops or a request falls outside their scope. That escalation handoff is the unglamorous feature that separates a credible enterprise product from a demo. It's also the piece that most pure-play agent startups have historically underinvested in, because it requires deep integration with ticketing, CRM, and workforce management systems rather than just a clever model.

The enterprise agent market won't be won by whoever has the smartest model. It will be won by whoever builds the most honest plumbing for the moments when the model shouldn't be in charge.

The timing matters. OpenAI is entering a crowded field — Salesforce, ServiceNow, Microsoft, and a constellation of startups are all selling variants of this vision — but it brings two advantages competitors can't easily match. First, distribution: ChatGPT is already embedded in many enterprises through existing licensing deals, so Presence isn't a cold sell. Second, model quality at the voice layer, which is where most customer-facing agents actually live. The GPT-Live voice stack gives Presence a credible real-time conversational baseline that text-first agent platforms have to bolt on awkwardly.

The risk is the same risk every enterprise AI play faces: trust is earned in deployment, not in the press release. Limited general availability is the right posture — it lets OpenAI co-build the integration depth with a small set of partners before broadening access. But the enterprises most likely to adopt early are also the ones with the most fragile customer relationships and the most aggressive procurement legal teams. If Presence agents go off-script in production, the cost isn't just a failed ticket — it's a board-level conversation about whether AI should be customer-facing at all. OpenAI's enterprise credibility will be built or broken on how those first deployments actually behave.
